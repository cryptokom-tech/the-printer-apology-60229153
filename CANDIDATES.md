# Candidates and local judging

Brief: one apology note (1–120 characters) from an office printer to
the people who jam it, feed it cheap paper and swear at it. Judge
order: (1) genuine printer apology with the printer's own gripes,
(2) a real laugh with the punch at the end, (3) printer truth
(jams, cheap paper, staples, colour), (4) freshness, (5) economy.

| # | Chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | 102 | Sorry about the jam. That sheet was damp, curled and stapled; I just finished what your paper started. |
| 2 | 98 | Sorry I chewed your last sheet. It was damp, curled, and stapled — honestly, it looked pre-chewed. |
| 3 | 91 | Sorry I printed 47 copies. You pressed Print like it owed you money; I panicked and obeyed. |
| 4 | 107 | Sorry I jammed. You fed me damp bargain paper and swore at me — I have boundaries, and they're tray-shaped. |
| 5 | 91 | Sorry I beeped for Tray 2 all night. I don't have a Tray 2. I just wanted someone to visit. |
| 6 | 96 | Sorry for the grey sunset. I'm a black-and-white printer; your colour photo was an act of faith. |
| 7 | 98 | Sorry I screamed 'PC LOAD LETTER.' I don't know what it means either; I was raised by error codes. |
| 8 | 75 | Sorry I jammed on one sheet. One. Even I'm embarrassed, and I have no face. |
| 9 | 80 | Sorry I ate the stapled original. I print paper, not scrap metal with ambitions. |
| 10 | 92 | Sorry for the paper jam. I saw the cheap damp stack coming and simply lost the will to feed. |
| 11 | 97 | Sorry I only jam when your boss watches. Even I get performance anxiety around quarterly reviews. |
| 12 | 98 | Sorry about the jam. The sheet was damp, curled and stapled — swear at the paper, it started this. |

## Judging

- #1 wins: a real apology, three printer truths in one breath
  (damp, curled, stapled — the cheap-paper stack the brief names),
  and the punch lands last as a blame-shift twist. It also avoids
  the eating-paper premise and the important-pages-jam angle.
- #2 is the closest rival, but the chew premise echoes an eating
  joke and the twist is weaker than #1's.
- #3 has a fresh simile and an end punch, but no jam or paper truth.
- #4 covers the swearing too, yet "boundaries" is generic office
  humour a deadpan judge has heard before.
- #5 is funny-sad with a weak grievance; #9's punch is strong but
  rests on one gripe and the same eating premise as #2.

Submitted line: #1.

## Re-check in this build session

Re-reading the twelve against the judge order, #1 still holds up:
it opens as a plain apology ("Sorry about the jam"), stacks three
printer-true grievances in the middle (damp, curled, stapled —
exactly the cheap-paper abuse the brief lists), and saves its joke
for the final clause, where the printer shifts blame to the paper
itself. #12 tries the same blame-shift but spends its punch on
"swear at the paper", which reads as an instruction rather than a
confession, so #1's deadpan "I just finished what your paper
started" lands cleaner. At 102 characters #1 also leaves headroom
under the 120 cap without padding, satisfying the economy check.
